The two minutes that decide it

  1. They search your organization's name. What comes back: your site, a registry, a complaint, or someone else with a similar name?
  2. They check the IRS record and a profile like Candid. Does the name, address and mission match your website?
  3. They open your site on a phone. Can they tell who you serve and what happens next, without hunting?
  4. They look for proof you are safe to fund: a privacy policy, board, finances, how donations are handled, a safeguarding policy if you work with young people.

Most small organizations fail on small things, not big ones: a mismatched name, a missing policy, a donate button that goes nowhere. Those are fixable in days.

Reasoned for your organization, not a template

Software gathers the evidence: crawl, public records, search results, listings. Then each finding is reasoned against your mission, population and funders, labeled by how sure we are, and given a change and a test for "done". A person reviews it before you see it.

Sample finding · fictional organization · not a real client

P0Teen intake form collects phone numbers with no privacy notice

What we saw: The "Join" form asks for name, date of birth, email and phone. No privacy text appears near the button and the privacy page does not mention the form.

Why it matters: The program serves 16 to 19 year olds. Funders and parents check how their data is handled, and the page promises something the form does not deliver.

Change: Remove date of birth until acceptance. Add two lines above the button on what is collected, who sees it and how to ask for deletion. Update the privacy page to match.

Done when: The form shows the notice, the privacy page lists this form, and a test submission is deleted. Legal review recommended for the final wording.

Certainty: verified on the live page. Owner: your team, then counsel.

How it works

  1. Free footprint scan. Send us your site. You get a short summary of what a funder would see, using public information only.
  2. Full audit. We review the site on real screens, check records and listings, grade 20 categories and write a 30, 60 and 90 day plan you can use with or without us.
  3. Repair sprint. We fix the urgent items in two to four weeks and show proof for each. Wording about donations, tax status and safeguarding waits for your written approval.
  4. Monthly care. We re-check, keep listings and content current, and tell you what changed.

What we need from you: access to your site, your approvals on sensitive wording, and real photos and numbers. We do not invent them.

Questions

Do you only work on websites?

No. The audit covers the site, your public records, listings and the legal basics around them. Site fixes are the repair sprint; listings and policies are checklists we help you complete.

We are fiscally sponsored. Does this work for us?

Yes. We check that the site describes the sponsor relationship accurately and that donation wording matches your agreement, and we ask for the sponsor's approval before it goes live.

What will the free scan use?

Your public website, public IRS data and ordinary search results. Nothing is published, and we do not share it.

Is the audit automated?

Software gathers evidence and an AI model helps reason about it. A person reviews every report. Where something needs a lawyer or accountant, we say so.

Where does this come from?

From launching a real youth nonprofit's site: fixing mismatched claims, missing safeguarding and privacy pages, form risks, search setup and mobile layout, then turning those lessons into a checklist.
