# Solo-operator price ladder for nonprofit audit and repair services

Research date: 2026-10-10. All sources were read as search excerpts only (untrusted data). The Firecrawl rate limit blocked full-page scrapes of Constructive, the Hyperakt home page, Connor Resource Associates and Luminea. Treat figures as indicative, and re-verify any figure before quoting it publicly. Two of the four example firms (Loop, Dalberg) were fully read; none of the four publishes prices.

## (b) Proposed ladder

| Tier | Low | Target | High | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Free footprint scan | $0 | $0 | $0 | It is a lead magnet. Hyperakt runs the same play with its "Evaluate Your Brand" brand-score page. |
| 2. Full audit (crawl, public records, SEO, legal basics, grant readiness, accessibility, 30/60/90 roadmap, funder checklist) | $750 | **$1,500** | $3,500 | Accessibility audits alone are $1,500-$3,500 (rows 12-13). Brand and readiness audits at the small end are $995 and $350-$1,200 (rows 8, 16). Your bundle spans four disciplines, so target the middle of the accessibility range. Use the high end for mid-size orgs with large sites. |
| 3. Repair sprint, 2-4 weeks (P0/P1 fixes) | $2,500 | **$5,000** | $9,000 | Rows 3-4 put freelancer and small-studio nonprofit work at $3K-$25K. Small orgs report $1K-$4K budgets (row 6). A fixed scope of top fixes sits well below a redesign and above what tiny orgs have spent. |
| 4. Monthly care | $250 | **$500** | $1,500 | Entry-level retainers run $1.5K-$2.5K per month for grant support (row 19). Monitoring tools alone cost $50-$300 per month (row 14). Cap hours (about 4-5 per month) so it stays below that entry retainer. |

Credit the audit fee against the sprint if the client signs within 30 days. This is common practice but not something the sources cover.

Sliding-scale rules, by annual budget. The multiplier applies to the target price. The sliding-fee idea is supported by row 24, but the exact thresholds below are my proposal, not sourced.
- Under $100K: 0.5x (audit $750, sprint $2,500). Cap the sprint at fewer fixes rather than discounting hours.
- $100K-$500K: 0.75x.
- $500K-$2M: 1.0x.
- Over $2M: 1.5x-2x, up to the high column.
- Fiscally sponsored project: price at the tier for the project's own budget, not the sponsor's. Invoice the sponsor, because sponsors usually take 8-10% (row 25) and the project needs an eligible budget line. Ask the sponsor whether they approve vendors.
- "Pay what the grant covers": the sources do not support it. Percent-of-award pricing (row 20) is contested, and grant professionals commonly avoid it. Use this substitute: quote a fixed fee, and itemize it as an allowable line in the client's grant budget. Offer a short-form quote the client can paste into proposals. Never make payment contingent on an award.

## (c) Risks

- **Budget reality.** Many targets have $1K-$4K or less to spend (row 6). Expect slow payment and grant-timing delays. Take 50% up front and consider invoicing at grant award.
- **Scope creep.** Define the P0/P1 list in the audit report, and make the sprint fixed-scope with a change-order price. Anything else goes to tier 4 or a new quote. Limit revisions to two rounds.
- **Liability.** State plainly that the "legal-basics gaps" and "grant readiness" sections are informational checklists, not legal, tax or accounting advice, and not a guarantee of funding or compliance. For accessibility, do not promise full WCAG conformance or ADA-lawsuit protection. Record each page's tested date. Use a written contract with limited liability and no outcome guarantees. Check whether you need professional liability insurance.
- **Sales tax and licensing.** Whether digital services are taxable, and whether you need a business license or registration, varies by state and city. Check locally. Nothing in this file is legal advice.
- **Evidence quality.** Most figures come from vendor blogs and anecdotes, with excerpt-level reads. They are marketing numbers, not transaction data. Re-test the price points by asking the first 5-10 prospects.
- **Percent-of-award and contingent fees.** Avoid them (see the "pay what the grant covers" note above).

## (d) How the example firms word and package offers

1. **Loop** organizes services into a few named, outcome-led packages (branding, website, annual reports), each with a "Process" page and a plain deliverables bullet list (accessibility is listed explicitly). We should do the same: name the tiers, list deliverables in bullets, and link a process page.
2. **Loop** speaks of "sustainable design partnerships" and "all sizes, issues, and complexities". Position the ladder as a path a small org can start on and grow with, not a one-off job.
3. **Hyperakt** offers a free "Evaluate Your Brand" brand-score as the front door and puts "Reach Out" as the only call to action. Our free scan plays the same role, with a single next-step button.
4. **Constructive** frames itself as "strategic partners" who co-create. Write offers in "we" language and show the client's role (who supplies access, approvals and content).
5. **Dalberg Design** sells a named method (human-centered design) with a clear iterative sequence and says it builds client capacity through toolkits. Our permanent funder checklist and 30/60/90 roadmap should be sold as the same kind of leave-behind, so the client learns the method.
